Vox

Privacy policy

Last updated: 27 September 2026

Vox is a free, open-source Windows app for voice dictation and meeting notes. It has no servers and no accounts of its own. The developer does not receive, collect or store any of your data.

Data stored on your PC

Your settings, Groq API key, dictation history, dictionary, meeting transcripts and notes are stored only on your computer, in %APPDATA%\Vox and in Documents\Vox Notes. Uninstalling Vox and deleting these folders removes them.

Groq

To turn speech into text and to clean up text and write meeting notes, Vox sends your recorded audio and the resulting text to Groq (groq.com) using your own Groq API key. When you take meeting notes, the meeting title and attendee names are included so that names are spelled and labelled correctly. Groq's handling of this data is covered by Groq's own privacy policy and terms.

Google Calendar (optional)

If you choose Connect Google Calendar, Vox requests read-only access to your calendar events (scope calendar.events.readonly) and your email address. Vox reads event titles, start and end times, attendee names and email addresses, the organizer, and video-call links, for the next 7 days. Vox uses this only to:

  • show your upcoming meetings in the app,
  • remind you (or start notes, if you turn that on) when a meeting begins,
  • title your notes and label who said what.

Calendar data is stored only on your PC. Meeting titles and attendee names are sent to Groq as described above, only for meetings you choose to record. Vox does not modify your calendar, does not share Google user data with anyone else, does not use it for advertising, and does not use it to train AI models.

Vox's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

To disconnect, click Disconnect in Vox > Notes > Calendar, or remove Vox at myaccount.google.com/permissions.

Recording other people

Meeting notes record what others say. Tell participants before you record. Recording laws differ by place.
